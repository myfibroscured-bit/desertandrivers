Find Yourself: 4 Days of Silence on the Path of Turquoise

Three nights under the stars on the way to the Temple of Hathor

This is not a yoga retreat. There is no teacher and no schedule. Just four days in the deep Sinai desert, three nights under the stars in its oldest and most silent places, and all the time you need to meditate, think, or simply be. Each night brings you closer to the Temple of Hathor, the “Lady of Turquoise”, where you watch the sunset, sleep under the stars and wake up to the sunrise. Joseph and Annet guide you, Chef Mary cooks every meal, and the desert does the rest.

The Path of Turquoise

The journey follows a story. You begin at the Nawamis, round stone tombs built about 6,000 years ago, older than the pyramids. Then you climb to an ancient Turquoise Cave, where the Egyptians mined the blue-green stone they offered to their goddess. And you end at her temple: Hathor, goddess of love, music and joy, and “Lady of Turquoise”, on a mountain top at Serabit el Khadim. Few places in Egypt are so old, so quiet and so rarely visited.

The journey

  • Day 1: Dahab → the Hiduda sand dune → lunch at Gebel Makhroum (Dragon Head Mountain) → the Nawamis. Afternoon, sunset and the night in silence at the Nawamis
  • Day 2: Sunrise meditation at the Nawamis → drive to the valley below the ancient Turquoise Cave → about an hour climbing up to the cave, time there in silence, and back down. Dinner and the night at our camp in the valley
  • Day 3: Drive to Serabit el Khadim. Lunch and a quiet rest in the shade. Around 15:00 we climb to the Temple of Hathor for sunset, then dinner, stargazing and the night at our camp near the temple
  • Day 4: Sunrise at the temple → walk down → lunch → drive back to Dahab, arriving in the late afternoon

We don’t tell you how to meditate. The desert is quiet enough to teach you.

The night sky, and the lights

Far from any town, Serabit el Khadim and the Nawamis have some of the darkest skies in Egypt. On a clear night you see the Milky Way from horizon to horizon. Over the years, some of our guests and local Bedouin have told us they saw strange lights moving in the sky here. We have not seen them ourselves, and we can’t promise anything. But Sinai has a long history of such stories, and in 2022 several US Army veterans said they saw unexplained lights over the Sinai border in 2014. Read the story

Bring with you

  • Your passport (checkpoints on the inland roads)
  • Good hiking shoes
  • Warm clothes for the nights (the mountains get cold)
  • A head torch
  • Anything that helps you meditate: a journal, a shawl, a cushion

Is this journey for you?

It is for people who want silence more than comfort. You sleep outside under the stars, there are no showers, and there are two steep climbs of about an hour each, to the Turquoise Cave and to the temple. You don’t need to be an athlete, but you should be able to walk uphill at your own pace. There is no programme to follow: join the walks, or stay at the camp and sit with yourself.
