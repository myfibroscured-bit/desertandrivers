Serabit el Khadim & Nawamis Day Trip from Dahab

The Temple of Hathor and the birthplace of the alphabet, in one day instead of two

High on a mountain in South Sinai stands the Temple of Hathor, and a short walk away is the cave where the first alphabet in history was found. Most companies need two or three days to bring you to Serabit el Khadim. We do it in one long, full day from Dahab, with the Nawamis tombs and our own Camel Rock on the way. Joseph and Annet guide you, and Chef Mary packs your lunch for the mountain.

Where our ABC began

Around 3,800 years ago, workers at the turquoise mines of Serabit el Khadim scratched short messages into the rock. They took Egyptian hieroglyphs and used them as letters for sounds. This script, Proto-Sinaitic, is the ancestor of almost every alphabet in the world, from Arabic and Hebrew to Greek and English. Flinders Petrie found the inscriptions, and the English Egyptologist Alan Gardiner decoded them. At Magharet el Teleha you stand in front of them.

The day

  • 06:00: pickup from your hotel in Dahab or from The Hub
  • About 1 hour: drive to the Nawamis, round stone tombs about 6,000 years old, older than the pyramids. About 30 minutes there
  • Camel Rock: on the road towards St Catherine: a big rock covered with Nabataean, Coptic and Greek inscriptions and camel carvings. Few people know it is there
  • About 3 hours: drive to Serabit el Khadim
  • About 1 hour: with lunch packed by Chef Mary, we climb to the Temple of Hathor, goddess of love and “Lady of Turquoise”. Time to explore the temple and enjoy the view
  • 30 minutes: hike to Magharet el Teleha, the cave of the first alphabet
  • About 1.5 hours: we hike down the mountain, stopping at Rod el-Eir: carvings of a giraffe, hieroglyphs, Proto-Sinaitic letters and a 3,000-year-old “hashtag” carved by the ancient Egyptians
  • About 3.5 hours: drive back. Around 20:00 drop-off at your hotel or at The Hub

Every letter you are reading right now has a grandparent carved into a rock at Serabit el Khadim.

This trip needs good physical strength: about three hours of hiking, with a steep climb up to the temple and a long walk down, plus about seven hours in the car. If you prefer a slower pace, ask us about doing it in two days.
